# The standard

Every module and every screen of One is held to the twenty points below. A
rule checks what a machine can, on every run; a person looks at what it
can't. Eleven points also hold a module before it is marked ready.

- `onedesk/one/standard.py` is the list in code. Each point's rules, by id,
  are in `docs/STANDARD-RULES.md`, generated by `scripts/docs.py`, and
  `python3 scripts/lint.py --point N` shows the same.
- `docs/READY.md` is how a module is finished and taken live.
  `docs/PASSOVER.md` is how a screen is walked against these points.
- A bad practice found on any screen gets a rule first, then the fix.

## Works

### 1. Demo and tests

The demo company holds a real case of everything the module does, and a
check or test covers every behaviour.

Why: a feature nobody can see in the demo is not finished, and a behaviour
no check runs breaks without anybody noticing. The demo is also what people
first see of One.

Held by: rules on demo coverage and install; before live, and a look at the
demo as its people use it.

- The screen's records exist in the demo company (`one_demo`), and the
  suite on it covers each kind, each branch, each preset, and each
  combination that matters (a project's kind against its sub-projects'
  kinds against how each bills).
- Each record is made by the person whose work it is, on the day it
  happened (`one_demo/history.py` moves it back).
- Every whitelisted method and OneAI tool is reached by a demo check, and
  each screen's walk adds a check that says what the walk found.
- **Watch for** demo data that lies: every open task overdue, every invoice
  due the day the demo was built. Fix the builder, then repair the site the
  same way, so a rebuilt demo and this one agree.

### 2. Rules on the server

Every rule lives on the record's own hooks (`validate`, `on_update`,
`on_submit`, `doc_events` in `hooks.py`).

Why: a list's bulk edit, the report view's inline edit and Data Import skip
the screen. A rule in an endpoint behind a button is a rule they get round.

Held by: rules on hooks and the ways round them, and OneGuard's rule that
a query takes what it is sent as parameters.

- Buttons and endpoints offer and orchestrate, never decide.
- The demo suite changes each rule every way: the screen, a bulk edit, a
  report cell and an import.

### 3. Hand-offs

Where the module's data comes from and where it goes in the other modules,
written down and run end to end.

Why: One is many modules on one set of records. A deal's customer is on the
project, its billing in OneBook, its items in OneInventory, its people in
OneHR, its tasks in OneTask. A break between two is found by nobody who
works in only one.

Held by: rules on Connections and the suite.

- The module's AGENTS.md (its README until it moves) has a **Connections**
  section, one line per module.
- The demo runs each hand-off end to end (`one_demo/check.py` HANDOFFS).
- The document a module hands on names where it came from at the top, by
  name, opening it.

### 4. What ERPNext already does

ERPNext handles more than a screen shows, and anything built on top keeps it
working.

Why: ERPNext refuses, or silently miscounts, what ignores its currency,
company, statuses and mappers. On OneProject this found euros read as
dirhams, a draft timesheet counted as cost and cancelled tasks counted as
planned hours.

Held by: rules on a screen's company and a chart's formats.

#### On every doctype

- **Currency.** A customer or supplier may be billed in their own currency,
  set by their receivable or payable account. A document made for them takes
  it (`get_party_account_currency`). Figures that add documents up use the
  `base_` amounts. A rate kept on our side says which currency it is in.
- **The company.** Every document has one, from the user's default (or the
  employee's, or the party's), with that company's accounts, warehouses and
  taxes. With one company nothing about it is shown; with several it is
  shown, filtered on and kept apart in every figure (CLAUDE.md, Several
  companies).
- **Draft, submitted, cancelled, amended.** Only submitted documents count.
  An amended document replaces the one it amends, and a revised quotation
  the earlier one, so neither is counted twice.
- **Statuses ERPNext moves itself** (Expired, Overdue, Lost, Ordered,
  Partially Ordered, Completed) are read, never worked out again.
- **One document made from another** goes through ERPNext's mapper
  (`make_sales_order`, `make_sales_invoice` and the rest), which keeps the
  links that ordered, billed and delivered amounts are counted from.
- **Taxes, price lists and discounts** come from the party and the item. A
  figure says whether it is before or after tax (we use net).
- **Precision and rounding** come from the field's precision through
  `flt(value, precision)`.
- **Dates** show in the user's date format and time zone, and money periods
  follow the fiscal year.
- **Record access.** A User Permission narrows every list, figure and link.
- **Units, stock and payment terms.** Quantities use the item's unit and
  conversion factor, a stock item moves stock, and an invoice's due dates
  come from its payment terms.
- **Watch for** ERPNext's own checks on save that a fix written straight to
  the database skips, such as a task's dates inside its parent's.

#### Figures that add things up

Set by the user on 2026-10-13, so it is never asked for again. A Home, a
card, a chart or a total:

- is the reader's company's (`erpnext.get_default_company`), in its
  currency, from amounts already in it (`base_` amounts, `stock_value`,
  `total_asset_cost`, a stock entry line's `amount`). Two companies in two
  currencies are never added up;
- adds a quantity up only within one item, with its unit. Across items a
  figure is worth, never pieces and metres in one bar;
- prints the way the reader and the workspace set it: the kit's `money`,
  `count`, `hours`, `quantity` and `weekdays`, months and days in the
  reader's language. Every frappe-ui chart and number card gets one of those
  formatters.

#### Money and tax

Set by the user on 2026-10-20: One is sold to any country, in any currency,
under any tax.

- An amount always carries its own currency; no heading names the currency
  so the figures under it need not.
- A generic label says **Tax**, **Tax ID** and **Tax Return**, never VAT or
  TRN. What really is one country's (the UAE VAT 201, the UAE tax-invoice
  block) is shown only where the workspace's country is that one.
- A figure with a tax on it, or one shown before tax, is underlined dashed
  and says the other side on hover: `other` on the kit's `Figures`,
  `head.taxed` on a desk record's head. `tests/guards/test_any_country.py`
  lists the regional files and each one's reason.

### 5. Settings and setup

One role-aware settings dialog per module, one foot, and what the setup
wizard may ask declared beside the module.

Why: a person looks for a module's settings in one place, and finds the same
place in every module. A new workspace is set up once, in the wizard, and
whatever can wait waits as a to-do.

Held by: rules on the foot and the setup steps; before live.

- The settings dialog (ControlCenter) leaves out what the reader's role
  doesn't allow, and ends with the centres that find the module: Roles and
  Access, Notifications, OneAI, Keyboard Shortcuts, Your Data.
- The foot is Settings, the tour, Help and the version
  (`docs/READY.md`, mechanism 5).
- `setup.py` declares each step, what can wait, and how to tell it's done
  (mechanism 9).
- A module with no window (`windowless`) or an engine says so in a sentence,
  and the rule skips it.

## Stays desk

### 6. Tier declared

How each record and each collection is shown is written down in
`onedesk/one/tiers.py`, with why.

Why: a record moves up a tier only when the one it has gets in the way of
real work. Written down, nobody draws a page of our own by habit, and no
view frappe offers is taken away.

Held by: rules on the tier registry.

- The record: **desk** (frappe's form as it is), **head** (the form with a
  Record Head), or **page** (a page of its own, for the few records people
  work inside all day). Today no record has a page of its own.
- Each collection view frappe has, as frappe's own or ours: List, Report,
  Kanban, Calendar, Gantt, Tree, Image, Dashboard, Map and Inbox.

#### A record answers first

Every record screen is checked against the record shell (`docs/SHELL.md`):

- **Its head answers what somebody opened it for**, above the fields and on
  every tab: the pill, one sentence when there is news, a band of metric
  cards, a chart only where the trend is the question, and its verbs, each
  asking in frappe's own dialog, at most one primary. A record with nothing
  worth saying gets no head; one that has gets a Record Head in its
  module's `heads.py`.
- **No form script draws a head** (`tests/test_head.py`). A script keeps
  only what is not a head: a dialog that answers inside itself, a sidebar
  action, a field's options.
- **A sentence that reads a field the form changes redraws as it changes**
  (`redraw_on`).
- **Another record's fields are edited in place**, as a Linked Section.
- **Mail, Files and Activity are the record's own tabs** (`one_record_tabs`).
- **A workspace administrator shapes it from the form's menu** (Customize),
  and OneAI proposes a change through the same door.
- **Lists stay frappe's list.** No stat strip above one.

### 7. Our look

The product has its own look, as OneMail and OneCloud do, built from
frappe-ui and espresso parts, and is walked in the browser.

Why: One is sold as one product, so every screen looks like it belongs.
Tokens keep light, dark and every theme right, and only a walk as each role,
on a desktop and a phone, sees a cut-off column or a crammed head.

Held by: rules on the frame, the tabs, the crumbs and the phone head; before
live, and a look at each screen as each role, desktop and phone.

- Nothing looks boxed in: sections sit on the page, and Save goes in the
  page head. Every colour, border and corner is a frappe-ui token (`--ink-*`,
  `--surface-*`, `--outline-*`, `--radius-*`), a border is 1px, and nothing
  of ours casts a shadow (`tests/guards/test_tokens.py`).
- Checked at 1440 and 390, light and dark. **Watch for** text cut off in a
  column, a figure only a bar says, a large empty state where every other is
  a line, and a list that only fits on a desktop.
- A list says more than a number.

#### One figure

Set by the user on 2026-10-20. A figure is one card everywhere: a small grey
label, the number at 20px, one line under it (a caption, or a change against
last month), 8px corners, with a bar or a sparkline below where there is
one. On the desk it is `frappe.ui.stat_card`; on an island frappe-ui's
NumberCard in the same frame, through the kit's `Figures`. A number that
warns is red-7. `tests/guards/test_figures.py` holds it.

#### A module's Home tells its story

Set by the user on 2026-10-13. A Home opens on figures with sparklines, a
button for each kind of thing that needs somebody, then the module's story
in parts, each a row of frappe-ui's charts (Number Card, Area, Bar, Donut,
Sankey, Heatmap, Funnel, Scatter, where each fits). A kind keeps its hue,
statuses keep theirs, magnitude runs light to dark, and no chart has two
value axes. Every figure is read as the reader may see it, and a slice or a
bar opens what it stands for. Today and Everything stay beside it as tabs.

#### A screen of our own follows an app people know

Set by the user on 2026-10-13. Before a screen is drawn, the app that does
the same job best is looked at (Shopify's Inventory and Katana's Stock for
what the stores hold) and the screen follows its layout. A list of records
is not a wall of text: a picture or avatar, the name with its code quiet
under it, one status as a coloured Badge, figures right-aligned with the
unit smaller and grey, a dash for nothing, and filters as a picker and
TabButtons above it. Its row in `docs/ISLANDS.md` names its model.

#### The panel

"It should be open on all collection views. On smaller devices it becomes
changeable from the breadcrumbs, like Projects ▾", and "make sure it's
universal over the app" (`public/js/crumb_switch.js`,
`tests/guards/test_record_page.py`).

- On a desktop the module's panel is open on every collection view (a list
  and its other views, a report, a page that lists). It is frappe's own
  sidebar, and a page leaves `hide_sidebar` unset.
- A record's page closes it (`hide_sidebar: !frappe.is_mobile()`).
- Every crumb with somewhere to go is a switcher with a search box, as a
  link field searches, at any width:
  - the place's crumb lists the module's places, read from the panel, in its
    order and under its sections, the place you are on marked;
  - a record's crumb lists records of its kind, its own suggestions first (a
    project's open projects), finds any by typing, and keeps **All …** at
    the foot;
  - a page gives the trail a `menu` and the switcher draws it; a page never
    draws a menu of its own on a crumb.
- On a phone the head is two bars, each its own height with a line under it
  (`public/js/phone_head.js`). The first has the panel's button, the place,
  the page's buttons and search. The second is the record's: its name, its
  status and a scope such as a project's All Work. A list's second bar has
  its view switch.
- A page that gathers several kinds of record keeps frappe's view switch
  (`public/js/page_views.js`).
- No module puts places of its own in the page head, and every view of a
  list marks the list's row in the panel.

#### A record's page

"Use frappe desk's sidebar, it's nicer and neater and cleaner on the right
side", and "move the vertical tabs to become horizontal tabs … don't make
them fixed". Every record on a page of its own (`one/tiers.py` PAGES) is laid
out as a frappe form is.

- Its details are frappe's own form sidebar on the right: the image, the
  name, print and like, Assign, Attachments, Tags, Share, and who last
  changed and made it. "Currently Viewing" is frappe's avatars in the head.
  The desk page names its record (`onedesk.island_page`'s `record`), and
  `onedesk.record.sidebar` lends frappe's sidebar the part of a form it
  reads. A page draws none of this itself.
- Its Attachments are one **Files** row with the count (`record_files.js`),
  opening the page's own Files view, or OneCloud on the record's folder.
- Its views are one row of tabs under its head (`frontend/src/kit/ViewTabs.vue`).
  When they don't fit, the row scrolls sideways, a fade shows where more
  continue, and the chosen tab is scrolled into view, after a reload too.
  The row moves with the page; it is never fixed to the top.
- On a phone frappe's sidebar is hidden, as on a form, and the tabs are the
  same row, swiped sideways.
- A place's crumb keeps its name and chevron whole at any width; the
  record's name after it is the one that shortens.
- Its Activity reads as a form's: frappe's own comment composer at the top
  (`frontend/src/kit/ActivityComposer.vue`), with @-mentions; under it the
  filters and **New Email**, which opens frappe's Email dialog on the record
  (`onedesk.record.email`). The list runs newest first with **Show earlier
  activity** at its foot, and each email has **Reply** and **Reply All**.
  A task's panel has the same.
- Every island sits inside frappe-ui's provider, so its messages and
  dialogs show, and its times are read in the site's timezone.

### 8. Nothing lost, and extensible

Everything frappe, erpnext and hrms give still reaches people, and what a
workspace adds still reaches the screen.

Why: the goal is every feature they ship, reached more simply. A page that
drops the form's menu, or a record type with no way in, takes away what
people paid for; a screen that ignores a Client Script breaks what the
workspace built.

Held by: rules on the record frame, the atlas and the places.

- A page of its own keeps everything frappe's form gives, through the
  record frame: the menu (Print, PDF, Email, Copy Link, Duplicate, Delete,
  Customize), the sidebar (Assign, Share, Like, Tags, Followers,
  attachments, who's viewing, created and modified by), the record kept live
  (`doc_subscribe`, `doc_update`), a save against the `modified` it loaded,
  and a warning before leaving unsaved work (`tests/guards/test_frame.py`).
- A Client Script runs on frappe's form. A page of its own declares its
  places, where OneStudio's extensions go, told what the view shows
  (`one_studio/places.py`). A place's notes and buttons are frappe-ui's
  Alert and Button (`PlaceSpot`). Server Scripts and our hooks run whatever
  the screen is.

#### Nothing upstream is lost either

Set by the user on 2026-10-13. Every record type, report and page frappe,
erpnext and hrms ship has a way in or a written answer (`docs/ATLAS.md`,
drawn by `scripts/atlas.py`).

- A way in is seen (a rail, a Home, one of our screens) or found (a form's
  link, its Connections tab, a list's Related Reports). A record on a page
  of our own has no Connections tab, so what only its form led to is placed
  again.
- Anything else is answered in `onedesk/one/atlas.py` as engine, inside,
  replaced or left out, or waits. What waits in each module is held to a
  count that only falls, and an upstream update that adds a record type
  fails until it is placed. **A module's walk is not done while it has
  anything waiting.**
- Nothing reached may be open to nobody, beyond each module's count.
- Each of our own screens is read for what its server lists: every record
  type it does not hear change, every number it prints bare, every company's
  record it lists without the reader's company, each held to a count.
- Five points are read for every record type a person reaches: point 1, the
  demo has one; point 12, a document sent outside prints from ours; point 7,
  its list says more than a number; point 13, its labels read in Arabic and
  German; point 11, what is sent about it is listed.

### 9. Built from frappe

Every part of a screen is taken from frappe first, and it behaves as frappe
does.

Why: frappe's parts carry its Link search, validation, translation,
formats and realtime. A part we write ourselves has to be kept up by us,
and falls behind.

Held by: rules on dialogs, buttons, lists, islands, formats and realtime.

- Each part comes from the first of these that has it, and is only written
  by us when none does:
  1. a **frappe-ui component** (`node_modules/frappe-ui/src/components/`);
  2. the **desk's own parts**: its controls, `FieldGroup`, `FilterGroup`,
     list and form views, dialogs, and espresso's `frappe.ui.button`,
     `badge` and `alert`;
  3. frappe's **primitives**: `frappe.call`/`xcall`, `frappe.realtime`,
     `frappe.utils`, `__()`, the model and the router.
- **How a part looks comes from frappe-ui.** Espresso's parts are frappe-ui's
  design ported to plain HTML, and the port is sometimes behind. Open the
  same component in frappe-ui (the `.vue`, its `.md` and its stories) and
  follow it where they differ in layout, variant, size or colour. Frappe-ui
  draws an alert with one action as one row; espresso put the button on a
  line of its own. The desk part stays; behaviour is still frappe's.
- Every value shown goes through `frappe.format` with its field. **Watch
  for** a number rounded by hand, a date written by hand, or an amount
  without its currency.
- Something on or off is a Switch, never a checkbox. A checkbox is only a
  step ticked off a checklist or a task done.

#### Everything follows frappe

How a screen looks can be ours; how it behaves is frappe's.

- **Fields** are frappe's own controls (`frappe.ui.FieldGroup`, or the desk
  form), never inputs drawn by hand.
- **A record being edited** acts like a desk form: it knows it is dirty by
  comparing against what was loaded, and is clean again when a change is
  undone; it warns before the page is left with unsaved changes; it saves
  against the `modified` it loaded; and it listens on the record's realtime
  room and says when somebody else changed it.
- **What a screen shows** changes live through `frappe.realtime` when the
  data under it changes, as the desk's lists do.
- Where frappe ships the behaviour, the screen uses it rather than a copy.

#### Changing a field where it is shown

Set by the user on 2026-10-12, first on the item page. A field on a page is
changed where it is shown, and it is the record's own form field.

- The page holds the record's real desk form out of sight
  (`onedesk.record.form`, the kit's `useForm`). Its client scripts, queries,
  `depends_on` and `set_df_property` all run.
- Each field shows its value as the form formats it, with a pencil (a
  chevron for a Link or Select) that floats the form's own control over the
  place (`onedesk.record.lend`, the kit's `FieldEdit`).
- A change saves the form as desk saves it. Enter or leaving the field
  saves, Escape reloads it unchanged, long text gets Save and Cancel, and a
  Check is frappe-ui's Switch.
- A field the form hides is not shown, and one it locks has no pencil.
- A table row's field is changed the same way (`onedesk.record.lend_row`),
  and a new row's fields go in together with Save and Cancel.
- Each label sits on the left and its value on the right (`Facts`).

### 10. Overrides recorded

Whatever of frappe's, erpnext's or hrms's we change is a row in
`scripts/overrides.py`, beside the upstream line it leans on.

Why: an upstream update that moves that line then breaks a test, not a
screen. `scripts/upstream.py` flags it at the next checkpoint.

Held by: rules on the ledger and the checkpoint.

- Native first: Custom Fields and Property Setters, then hooks, then a Record
  Head or a form script, and a page of its own last.
- `python3 scripts/overrides.py --check` passes.

## Speaks

### 11. Notifications

The module tells people what they need, on the channels that make sense,
only to the roles that should hear it.

Why: a notice nobody needed teaches people to ignore the next one, and one
sent to the wrong role leaks. NotificationCenter is where each person
chooses, so every type has to be declared there.

Held by: rules on declared types, channels, the one layout and the one
sender; before live, and a look at what each event sends.

- Every type is declared in the module's `notifications.py`, with its
  channels (bell, email, push, or a sentence saying why not) and its sender
  (`system` or `workspace`), sent through the hub on one layout, in the
  reader's language (`docs/READY.md`, mechanism 8).
- The workspace changes its own copy in Settings › Notifications.
- **Watch for** amounts in the site's currency instead of the company's, a
  notice sent to whoever did it, and a notice sent during an install, a
  migrate or an import.

### 12. Printed

A record that leaves the company (a quotation, an order, an invoice, a
payslip, a report) prints on a format we ship.

Why: what a customer or supplier holds is One's face outside the workspace,
and ERPNext's own formats look like ERPNext.

Held by: rules on the shipped formats and the print blocks.

- A format is laid out in frappe's print format builder from its own pieces
  (One Sales Invoice is the first), or, until redrawn, from the print blocks
  (`one_studio/print_blocks.py`, `templates/one_print/`).
- What a native format cannot reach becomes a field on the record, never a
  block.
- A format's `modified` moves with every change, or a migrate leaves the
  site's copy as it was.

### 13. Plain words

Every heading, note, button, column, badge, empty state and message reads
like a good SaaS product, in English, Arabic and German.

Why: the words are a product reporting a fact. A thing called by ERPNext's
name is what Ctrl+K finds, and a coinage is a word nobody can search for.

Held by: rules on headings, descriptions, banned words, translations and
names; before live, and a look at every string in all three languages.

- `docs/WORDING.md` and the `wording` skill are the rules. A heading is five
  words or fewer; a description is one sentence; a note is optional and one
  short sentence.
- A thing is called what ERPNext calls it: a rail row, heading, crumb, label,
  column and status say the doctype's, report's, field's or status's own
  name (Purchase Receipt, not Receipts). Frappe's words, never our coinages
  ("carry", "came with", "take back").
- No colon or semicolon joining two clauses, and no narrating how OneAI
  works.
- Every string is in Arabic and German before it is committed, with the
  glossary's word for each term (`onedesk/locale/glossary.csv`), and none
  of a module's strings left in `onedesk/locale/glossary_debt.json`.
- No sentence of frappe's, erpnext's or hrms's names them:
  `onedesk/translations/` says One instead, but for the outside names kept
  on purpose.

## Knows

### 14. Documented, with Help and a tour

`AGENTS.md` for agents, a `docs/` wiki for people shown in Help, and a
first-visit tour of each area of the screen.

Why: OneAI's `how_to` answers from the Help pages, so what Help doesn't say
OneAI can't either. A tour shows a newcomer where things are, and is run
again from the foot.

Held by: rules on the folder, the Help pages and the tours; before live.

- Each screen has its Help page or section, written for the people using
  it: what it is for, how to fill it in, and who sees and changes what
  (`docs/READY.md`, mechanism 4).
- A Help page names no code, its headings are five words or fewer, and its
  pictures sit beside it.
- `tour.py` has a tour of each area, each step a `data-tour` mark, a heading
  of five words or fewer and one sentence (mechanism 10).

### 15. Versioned, with What's New

Every change to a module is a version, with a What's New entry written for
its people, opened from the version at its foot.

Why: people learn what changed from What's New, not from us. A version that
names a file, or a change with no version, leaves them guessing.

Held by: rules on the fingerprint, the numbering and the words; before live.

- A version is major, minor or fix, by the biggest change in it
  (`docs/READY.md`, Which number moves), and a day's work in a module is one
  version.
- Its words are English, for the people using the module, with no file,
  function or commit, and a title of five words or fewer.
- The version at the foot has a dot until seen, and opens UpdateCenter.

### 16. OneAI knows and does

OneAI answers from the module's Help, reads what the reader sees, and does
what the page does as a card the reader approves.

Why: OneAI is one way into everything. A thing the page does that OneAI
can't, with no reason given, is a gap; a card that bypasses the page's code
is a hole.

Held by: rules on reach and how AI is drawn; before live.

- Every call that changes something is in the module's `agent.py`, in `REACH`
  naming the tool whose card does it, or in `NOT_FOR_AI` with the reason in
  one sentence (`docs/READY.md`, mechanism 11).
- Approving a card runs the page's own code as whoever pressed Approve, so
  it is refused exactly where the page would refuse. Nothing changes until
  it is approved.
- OneAI knows which screen the reader is on and what records that means,
  and offers the screen's own suggestions if any are worth offering.
- Whatever is AI is drawn one way: what OneAI does or did is
  `onedesk.oneai.tag` ("Read by OneAI"), and what asks it is
  `onedesk.oneai.button`, opening the panel on the same question. A violet
  badge or a plain button standing in for one is a finding.

### 17. Intake

Which mails and files should make or change the module's records, and
whether Intake does.

Why: most records start as somebody else's document, a bill, a CV, a bank
statement. Intake only learns what it is shown.

Held by: rules on the samples and the suite.

- Each has a real sample in `tests/samples/intake/<module>/`, and the demo
  suite has Intake read it.

## Rules

### 18. Permissions and roles

Who sees the screen, who may change what, and the server holds that line.

Why: a button hidden by the screen is still a call anybody can make. Roles
that ship with the module, changed in AccessCenter, mean a workspace never
starts open to everybody.

Held by: rules on shipped roles, grants and System Manager, and OneGuard's
rules on what a call checks, which method may change data, secrets in the
code and what a guest is given; before live.

- Each screen is walked as each person who uses it.
- The module's roles come from its `access.py`; one person given more on
  one record is a `rights.grant`, never a share written by hand
  (`docs/READY.md`, mechanism 7).
- Only AccessCenter draws the role matrix, and only NotificationCenter a
  person's choices (mechanisms 14 and 15).
- Nothing waits for System Manager: a space's rail opens for its own roles.

### 19. Legal

Every fact about what the module does with data that the agreements must say
is declared in its `trust.py`, beside the code.

Why: what people agree to is assembled from these, so the agreements can't
say other than the code does. No rule can read whether a clause is true, so
a person reads it against the code.

Held by: rules on assembly, revisions, hosts and promises; before live, and
a look at what it does with data against what it says.

- A `clause()` for the Privacy Policy, the Terms, the Data Processing
  Addendum or the AI Addendum, and a `subprocessor()` for any company it
  sends data to. TrustCenter assembles them (`trust_center/AGENTS.md`).
- A changed clause fails until it is a new revision (people agree again) or
  a recorded hash (a typo). A host the code calls that no `subprocessor()`
  names fails.
- Everything optional it sends out is a `service()`, off until an
  administrator agrees. Clauses follow the legal copy rules and name the
  laws they answer, and TrustCenter shows all of it (mechanisms 6 and 13,
  and the `legal` skill).

### 20. One home, clean code

All of the module is in its folder, in one layout, with each concern in its
place.

Why: a module that lives in one place is versioned, read and handed to an
agent whole. A file per concern means a notification, a role or a OneAI
tool is found where every other module keeps it.

Held by: rules on the folder, the layout and lint; before live.

- Everything is in the module's folder, apart from what frappe forces
  elsewhere: `public/js/<module>/`, `public/css/<module>/`, its screens in
  `frontend/src/`, fixtures and translations (`docs/READY.md`, A module's
  folder).
- No Markdown in it but `AGENTS.md`, `SCRATCHPAD.md` and `docs/`.
- Logic, endpoints, notifications (`tell.py`), roles (`access.py`), legal and
  AI (`agent.py`) each in their place. A helper said once.
- Lint clean (`ruff`). No comment in code but what CLAUDE.md allows; the why
  goes in the commit, AGENTS.md or a docstring.
