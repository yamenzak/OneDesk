- **Several companies.** With more than one company, invoices, payments,
  journal entries, bank records, accounts and the rest show their
  **Company**, starting as yours, and every list opens on your company.
- A purchase invoice, a payment, a journal entry, a bank transaction, a bank
  account and a supplier say what matters at the top, as a sales invoice
  does, and their lists count their records by status or kind.
- Home's **Cash** and **Profit This Month** count only what you may see.
- An overdue invoice's amount is in the currency it is owed in.
- The Monday mail of bills due comes once for each company, in its
  currency.
- **Owed to Us** and **We Owe** take off credit and debit notes not yet set
  against an invoice, as Accounts Receivable and Payable do.
- What a customer or supplier owes, on their page and on an invoice, is
  your company's only, in the currency it is owed in.
- A bank account's **Balance** is in the account's own currency.
- A bank line suggests only a payment into or out of its own account.
- An invoice made from a copy keeps a bank account of its own company to
  be paid into.
- On the portal, a customer or supplier can print an invoice but never the
  voucher with its accounts, and **Paid** on an invoice in another currency
  is right.
- Setting a bill's recoverable VAT for the return leaves a note on it, and
  never changes a bill in a period the books are locked for.
- On Home, **I** starts an invoice, **B** a bill, **P** a payment and **J**
  a journal entry.
- A sales invoice, a purchase invoice and a payment have a short tour of
  their own, and Help has pictures of each screen.
- Home's figures keep their whole titles on a narrow screen.
- A payment received against an invoice says nothing needs doing.
- With more than one company, **Books Check** names the company it checks.
- In OneCalendar, **Invoices Due** and **Bills Due** put what is still owed on the day it falls due.
