What {one_ai} reads is now handed to each app's agent: {one_book}'s accountant, {one_crm}'s sales assistant, {one_hr}'s people officer, {one_inventory}'s storekeeper and {one_project}'s coordinator each make their own records, under their own name and with **Undo**, and each one's actions are on its tab in {ai_center}. Every record starts as a draft. What an agent may not make itself, such as a contract, waits for you as a suggestion. Turning agents off in {ai_center} stops them, and {one_ai} still reads.

{one_quality}'s quality inspector, {one_factory}'s production planner and {one_display}'s screen keeper now work too, on their own app's records, each with its actions on its tab in {ai_center}. The privacy policy and the subprocessors list now say Cloudflare also keeps the names of the records an agent was handed.

When you ask in {one_ai}'s panel, the agent of the app you are in answers, with that app's tools alone; elsewhere Una answers, and asks the right agent for you. An agent turned off in {ai_center} no longer answers.

What waits for your decision is now a card in {one_chat}, in your conversation with the agent that did it, with **Approve** and **Dismiss**; something done that the auditor doubts has **Keep** and **Undo**. **Documents** is gone: **Actions**, first in {one_ai}'s rail, lists everything {one_ai} and the agents did for you, and **Explain** and **Pay** stay beside the file in {one_cloud} and under the message in {one_mail}. If you administer the workspace, each agent's tab in {ai_center} shows what waits on a person and the documents it read.

{one_ai}'s page in {one_marketplace} shows it at work, in pictures and short clips of the demo company.

{ai_center} has a mark of its own: {one_ai}'s hexagon inside {one}'s ring.

{setup_center} has an agent of its own, its setup guide. It holds no role, so whatever it would change is a card for you to approve.

In {automation_center}, **Hand to an Agent** gives what started an automation, such as a new message, to an app's agent with what to do with it. The agent works on it as if you had asked it yourself, at its own levels, so what it may not just do waits for you as a card.

A message {one_mail} holds back as risky is read only once someone releases it, and one {one_ai} reads as phishing or spam adds that to the message's risk, so {one_mail} may hold it.
