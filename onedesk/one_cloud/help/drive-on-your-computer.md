# {one_cloud} as a Drive

You can open a {one_cloud} folder as a network drive on your computer, and work
on its files in Word, Excel or any other app.

## Connecting a Drive

1. Right-click a folder and choose **Connect as a drive…** (or right-click an
   empty space for the current folder).
2. Copy the address and add it as a drive: *Map network drive* on Windows,
   *Connect to Server* on macOS, *Other Locations* on Linux.
3. Sign in with your email and a drive password. Press **Create password**,
   name the computer, and copy the password. It's shown only once.

## Drive Passwords

Create one password per computer. Each is listed with when it was last used,
and the cross removes one without affecting the others. A drive password
only opens your drive. It doesn't work for {one} in a browser or for anything
else.

## Working on the Drive

The drive works like any other: open, save, drag in, rename, create folders
and delete. It shows exactly what you can open in {one_cloud}. Saving over a
file keeps the old one as a version, and deleting moves it to the Recycle
Bin. A document, workbook or presentation made in {one} shows as a Word, Excel
or PowerPoint file, and saving it there saves it back into {one}.

## When Two People Open It

While you have a file open from the drive in Word, Excel or LibreOffice,
nobody else can change it: their app opens it read-only and says it's
yours, and {oneos} and the editors in {one_cloud} do the same. If somebody is
editing it in {one_sheet}, {one_doc} or {oneos}, your app opens it read-only
and says who. Your saves while it's open are one version.

An app that doesn't hold the file open, such as one on a phone, never saves
over a newer version. If the file changed after you opened it, your save is
kept next to it, named for you, such as *Budget (Huda's copy).xlsx*, and
your next saves go into that copy. If somebody moves a file you have open to
the Recycle Bin, you're told.
