{one_mail} now checks every message that arrives for the signs of fraud: a sender the receiving server couldn't confirm, a link that shows one website and opens another, an attachment that can run macros or programs, or a domain that looks like a customer's, a supplier's or your own. A risky message shows a warning above it saying why, and which domain it imitates. One that looks dangerous waits in **Held**, under the Inbox, where no rule, reply or agent sees it until you **Release** or **Delete** it. A Mail Manager sets when mail is warned and held in **Settings › Risky Mail**. The privacy policy now says what is checked, that nothing of it leaves the workspace, and that what it found is kept with the message.

A mail rule that replies with {one_ai} now has {one_mail}'s receptionist write the reply. It counts against the receptionist's credits for the month in {ai_center}, and while agents are turned off there, such a rule sends no reply.

{one_mail}'s page in {one_marketplace} shows it at work, in pictures and short clips of the demo company.

Your mail rules now run from {automation_center}, each as it was, in the same order, on or off as it was, and one waiting for approval still waits. **Automation** in a mailbox's settings lists them and opens each one there. Where {one_ai} may read the mailbox, a rule can also hand a message to an app's agent, such as {one_book}'s accountant, with what to do with it.
